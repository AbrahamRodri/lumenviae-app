# Lumen Viae

> *"Light of the Way"* — a Catholic Rosary meditation and prayer companion for iOS.

Lumen Viae guides you through the Rosary with meditations drawn from the saints,
keeps the days you pray, and carries a small library of Catholic devotion around
it: the 33-day consecration to Mary, the 1962 Missal and the Divine Office, a
prayer book, a library of chant, and a shelf of spiritual classics. It is built
for the quiet: dark, gold-lit, and free of anything that hurries you.

---

## What the app does

### Pray the Rosary

Pick a set of mysteries — Joyful, Sorrowful, Glorious, Luminous, or the Seven
Sorrows of Mary — then the way to pray them. The Rosary comes in three forms,
chosen on the mysteries' page: with a meditation from the saints read between
the decades, as the **Scriptural Rosary** with a Bible verse for every Hail
Mary, or as **The Rosary Said Aloud**, with nothing read between. Each opens on
one page, *Your Rosary Today*, which confirms two choices above **PRAY**:

- **Audio** — *Meditation Only* (in the Scriptural Rosary, *Read in Silence*)
  or *Whole Rosary*, every prayer said aloud.
- **Counting** — *On the Screen* or *On My Rosary*, asked only while the voice
  reads the meditation alone; when it says every prayer, it moves the beads.

Home proposes the day's mysteries on the traditional schedule, Sunday following
the season, or on the modern one (the Luminous on Thursday) if you choose it in
Settings. The raised **Pray** button runs your chosen act in one tap (today's
Rosary unless you pick another); press and hold it for a tray of the devotions
you keep.

The player sets each mystery under its painting, the meditation narrated in a
choice of voices at a speed you set (0.7× to 1.7×), with a text-only reader a
tap away. The whole Rosary hangs as one strand of beads at the right edge: swipe
down a bead at a time, and the decade turns on its own at the next Our Father.
On each mystery the strand opens once the meditation has been heard. Counted
*On My Rosary*, the player steps a mystery at a time with arrows, for a hand
that keeps its own count. An unfinished Rosary is offered back on home at the
bead where it stopped.

With the **Whole Rosary**, every prayer and announcement is said in your
narration voice, and in the Scriptural Rosary each bead's verse too; the beads
move with the voice, so the Rosary can be prayed with the phone in a pocket. Its
recordings are fetched in your voice and kept, and an interrupted Rosary resumes
on the prayer where it stopped. The Seven Sorrows is the Servite chaplet: seven
sorrows of seven Hail Marys, without the Fatima Prayer, and said aloud it opens
with the Act of Contrition and closes with three Hail Marys for Our Lady's
tears. Settings → After the Rosary adds prayers for the Pope's intentions, the
Memorare and the Saint Michael Prayer after the closing prayer.

### The Scriptural Rosary and the Rosary Said Aloud

The Scriptural Rosary sets a verse of the Douay-Rheims beside every Hail Mary,
all 249 verses bundled in the app, on the same strand of beads the player hangs.
It can be read in silence, said aloud, or counted on your own rosary a mystery
at a time. **The Rosary Said Aloud** prays on the same screens with nothing read
between the decades, every prayer said by the voice and set in full as it is
said.

### Choose your meditations

Meditation sets come from the Lumen Viae API, listed under *Meditations* on each
mysteries' page as a gallery or a ruled list, filtered by label (Inside the
Scene, Saints, Reflections, Gospel, and any others the server adds), and pinned
to the top at will. Authors include St. Alphonsus Liguori, St. John Henry
Newman, Bl. Anne Catherine Emmerich, Ven. Fulton J. Sheen, and others.

### Consecration to Mary

A guided 33-day preparation after the method of St. Louis de Montfort, with a
tab of its own, **Consecrate**. Choose a Marian feast, and the app counts back
33 days of preparation ending on its eve, leaving the act of consecration for
the feast itself. Each day has readings from Scripture, the *Imitation of
Christ* and *True Devotion*, the prayers of its period (in English, Latin, or
both), and a journal prompt. The Veni Creator, the Ave Maris Stella (in its
feast-day melody), the Magnificat, the Glory Be and the litanies of Loreto and
of the Holy Name can be heard sung, from the Chant Library.

### Home, Explore, and the Chapel

The app has four tabs — **Home**, **Consecrate**, **Prayers** and **Chapel** —
beside the raised Pray button.

Home holds the day's mysteries; a **Rosary Mysteries** grid of the week's
mysteries and the Seven Sorrows (the Joyful, Sorrowful and Glorious on the
traditional schedule, with the Luminous after the Glorious on the modern one);
and **Today's Prayer**: the Consecration to Mary and the hour's order from the
Prayer Book, then the day's feast over **Today's Mass** and the **Hours of
Prayer**. A Spiritual Reading shelf and a quote close the page. Settings and
About stand in the masthead; the search glass opens **Explore**, which browses
Rosary Mysteries, Prayers, Chant, Today in the Church, Spiritual Reading and
Learn More, and searches mysteries, library pages, short readings, prayers,
chants, the journal and every meditation set at once.

**The Chapel** tab is a page you arrange in place: the next of your **Daily
Prayers** at the top, then tiles to reorder, set full or half width, or put away
in a tray — **Today**, **Prayer Streak**, **Consecration**, **Prayers**,
**Reading**, **Chant**, **Reflections**, **Today in the Church** and
**Library**. Long-press to arrange.

### Today's Mass and the Hours of Prayer

**Today's Mass** is the Daily Missal: the 1962 propers for any day, in Latin,
English, or both, with the whole Ordinary laid through them or the propers
alone, from the Missale Meum API. The **Hours of Prayer** are the Divine Office,
the Breviarium Romanum under the 1960 rubrics: eight hours in plain names, from
the Night Vigil to Bedtime Prayer, opening on the hour it is now. Its texts come
from the app's own `/office` API, which assembles them from a self-hosted
Divinum Officium engine. Both keep the days they fetch and fetch the coming days
ahead.

### Prayers

The Prayer Book is a tab of its own: more than 150 of the Church's common
prayers, bundled, in English and, where the Church prays in Latin, in Latin too.
It opens on a search, then three parts: **Today**, the prayer for the hour over
its painting, Our Lady's prayers led by the antiphon the season sings, and the
prayers you have saved; **Occasions** — At Mass, At Confession, At Home, In
Need — with the orders of prayer kept there; and **All Prayers**, twelve
chapters by topic, from Basic Prayers to Short Prayers.

Eleven orders of prayer are said together, among them Morning Prayers, the
Angelus and Night Prayers, and an order is prayed along one prayer at a time,
in silence or aloud in your narration voice. A prayer can be saved, heard in
chant where it has one, or learned by heart in four steps: Read, Some hidden,
First letters, By heart. The Angelus gives way to the Regina Caeli in
Eastertide, and the Angelus bell (Settings → Devotion) rings at six, noon and
six.

### The Chant Library

A hundred and four chants, each with its recording, its score and its words in
Latin and English, timed line by line and bundled so they sound without a
connection. The library has six ways in — **Today** (led by tonight's song to
Mary), **Seasons**, **Occasions**, **Types**, **Learn** and **Saved** — a search
across titles, words and the prayers sung, and a mini player at the foot of
every section.

A chant's page shows the score (pinch to zoom) and the words; it slows to
three-quarter speed, repeats, steps a line at a time, takes turns with the
singer, and stops on a sleep timer. **Learn this chant** teaches it in four
steps: Listen, Read along, Sing along, On your own. Favourites and your own sets
of chants are kept under Saved. The recordings and scores are Verbum Gloriae's,
shared under their copyleft licence.

### Spiritual reading

Four public-domain classics — *The Imitation of Christ*, *The Story of a Soul*,
the *Confessions* and *The Dolorous Passion* — are fetched from Project Gutenberg
the first time each is opened, cut into chapters on device, and kept. Three have
LibriVox recordings matched to their chapters, which can be saved for listening
offline. A passage can be kept as a note in the journal or marked as a place to
come back to. The complete *True Devotion to Mary* (Faber's 1862 translation) is
bundled, with your place and your marks kept.

### Journal and Progress

The journal keeps reflections written at a prayer's Amen or after a consecration
day, notes from a book, and free-standing entries: searchable, editable, and on
device only. It opens from the Chapel's Reflections tile and from Explore's
search. The Prayer Record keeps streaks, history and milestones, reached from the
Chapel's Prayer Streak tile and from Settings → Devotion. Everything prayed
counts on one prayer day, which turns at 4 AM. Milestones follow the Church's
devotional structures rather than scores — 3 days, a triduum; 7, a faithful
week; 9, a novena; 33; 54, a Rosary novena; 100; and 365, a year of grace — and
nothing in the app shames a missed day.

### Resource library

Reached from Explore and the Chapel's Library tile; each short reading opens as a
page of its own, leading on to a feast, a prayer, or the next reading.

- **How to Pray** — a course for someone who has never prayed the Rosary: three
  lessons (the beads and the order, the prayers, the mysteries), then *Your
  First Rosary*, a guided Rosary with every prayer in full.
- **The Mysteries in Scripture** — each mystery's passage and key verse.
- **The Marian Library** — the next feast of Our Lady, the four Marian dogmas,
  Mary in Scripture, approved apparitions, the Marian saints, the Rosary through
  history, and the titles of Our Lady.
- **St. Carlo Acutis** — his life, his rule of life, and a votive candle.
- **The Devotion in Summary** — a digest of *True Devotion*.

### Making it yours

- **Three themes** — Marian Blue, Midnight, Candlelit (the default) — applied live.
- **Prayer language** — English, Latin, or bilingual in either order.
- **Text size** for the app, with separate sizes for the liturgy and the shelf;
  the phone's Larger Text setting is followed up to Extra Large.
- **Daily reminders** with a choice of bundled sounds, at a time you pick. The
  copy follows what you said drew you to the Rosary
  (`app/Data/ReminderMessages.swift`) and never mentions streaks.
- **Offline downloads** — every meditation set with its painting and narration,
  and the spoken Rosary's and the Prayer Book's recordings. With them and the
  bundled prayers, verses, chants and books, the Rosary prays without a
  connection; the Missal, the Office and the shelf keep whatever they have fetched.
- **A first-run introduction** of nine slides, skippable, asks what brings you to
  the Rosary, what you'll hear, where you'll count (not asked when the whole
  Rosary is said aloud), and your colours, language and reminder; each can be
  changed later in Settings. A five-step tour then points out Home's controls
  once, and after an update a **What's New** sheet says once what changed.

## Architecture

SwiftUI throughout; the one UIKit view is the system mail composer behind Send
Feedback. iOS 17.0 minimum. The module compiles with default `@MainActor`
isolation and approachable concurrency, so types are main-actor isolated unless
marked otherwise; API models carry `nonisolated` Codable conformances because
offline reads decode them off the main actor.

```
app/
├── appApp.swift              @main entry, SwiftData container
├── ContentView.swift         Root: tab switch + NavigationStack
├── Navigation/ ViewModels/   AppRouter and AppRoute; @Observable view models
├── Models/                   API and SwiftData models, the Rosary's forms, the
│                             strand, the spoken script, Chapel tiles, chants
├── Views/
│   ├── Home/                 Home, Explore, all mysteries
│   ├── Meditation/ Prayer/   The shelf, Your Rosary Today, the player
│   ├── Scriptural/           The Scriptural Rosary (and the Rosary Said Aloud)
│   ├── Chapel/               The arrangeable page and its tiles
│   ├── Consecration/         33-day preparation (its own NavigationStack)
│   ├── TrueDevotion/         Book reader
│   ├── Library/              Spiritual Reading: shelf, book, chapter reader
│   ├── PrayerBook/           The Prayers tab: Today, Occasions, chapters,
│   │                         praying along, learning by heart
│   ├── Chant/                The Chant Library, a chant's page, its score
│   ├── Resources/            How to Pray, the guided Rosary, In Scripture, the
│   │                         Marian Library, Carlo Acutis, the Missal, the Office
│   ├── Journal/ Progress/ Account/ Onboarding/ Launch/
│   ├── FirstUseTour/ WhatsNew/  Home's first tour; the sheet after an update
│   └── Me/                   The rule editor, the Pray button's editor and tray
├── Components/               Tab bar, header, cards, the bead strand
├── DesignSystem/             Theme, fixed colours, Typography, AppIcon, Motion,
│                             ReadingText
├── Data/                     Bundled prayers, mysteries, verses, consecration,
│                             the Prayer Book (PrayerBook/), the chant catalog
├── Services/                 API clients, audio, caches, offline, settings
└── Resources/                Fonts, True Devotion JSON, reminder sounds, chants
```

### Where the data lives

**The Lumen Viae API** (`https://lumenviae.fly.dev/api`) is a companion Phoenix
app that owns the meditation content, so it can change without an app release:

| Endpoint | For |
|---|---|
| `GET /meditation-sets?category=` | The sets for one set of mysteries |
| `GET /meditation-sets/:id` | A full set: meditations, painting, narration links |
| `GET /meditations/:id/audio` | A freshly signed narration link (`?voice=`) |
| `GET /voices` | The narration voices, default first |
| `GET /rosary/audio` | The spoken Rosary's and the Prayer Book's recordings for one voice |
| `GET /office/…` | A day, an hour, or a month of the Divine Office |
| `POST /completions` | An anonymous note that a Rosary was finished |

The server also offers GraphQL and a versioned JSON:API at `/api/v2`; the app
calls neither yet.

**Elsewhere** — the Missal from the Missale Meum API, the shelf's texts from
Project Gutenberg and its recordings from LibriVox. The Scriptural Rosary's
verses came from thedouayrheims.com, the chants from Verbum Gloriae and the
paintings from Wikimedia Commons, at generation time; the app never calls them.

**Bundled** — every Rosary prayer in English and Latin, the mysteries themselves
(names, scripture, descriptions and fruits, in `Data/MysteryData.swift`; the app
does not fetch them), the Scriptural Rosary's verses, the 33 days of preparation
and the day of consecration, *True Devotion*, the Prayer Book, the Chant
Library's recordings, scores and timed words, one Luminous meditation set that
prays with no signal, the resource pages and the quotes.

**On device** — SwiftData holds prayer sessions, journal entries, consecration
progress, and reading progress in *True Devotion* and the shelf. `UserDefaults`
holds settings, pinned sets, the unfinished-Rosary snapshot, the Chapel's layout,
the Prayer Book's saved and learned prayers, and the Chant Library's favourites
and sets. Application Support holds the offline sets, narration and paintings,
the Missal, Office and Library caches, and the spoken Rosary's and the Prayer
Book's recordings, all excluded from backup.

## Building

Requires Xcode 26 or later. Open `app.xcodeproj` and run the `app` scheme; the
deployment target is iOS 17.0. No package in the app (the tests take one,
swift-snapshot-testing), no secrets, and nothing is generated at build time. The generated content is checked in, made by the
scripts in `Tools/`: `Data/ScripturalRosaryData.swift`;
`Data/ChantCatalogData.swift`, folding in the timed lines from
`Tools/ChantLines/`; the recordings and `.lvscore` scores in
`Resources/Chants/`; the paintings fetched by `Tools/ContentSourcing/`; and
`Resources/TrueDevotionBook.json`. The Prayer Book in `Data/PrayerBook/` is
written by hand, and `Tools/PrayerBook/` exports its words for the server to
record. New `.swift` files under `app/` are picked up by Xcode's
file-system-synchronized groups; there is nothing to add to the project file.
The unit tests, Swift Testing suites in `appTests/`, run from the same scheme
(Product → Test, or `xcodebuild test`); there are no UI tests. The snapshot
suite in `appTests/Snapshots/` compares the main screens with PNG baselines
recorded on the iOS 26.2 simulator (skipped on other runtimes); after a
deliberate visual change, run the tests with
`TEST_RUNNER_SNAPSHOT_TESTING_RECORD=failed` set, review the new PNGs and
commit them.

The API is public. The app's only write is the anonymous completion, sent when a
Rosary prayed with meditations is finished; it carries the set and whether the
whole Rosary was said aloud, and no identifier. A debug build can point the
Lumen Viae API at another server with the `LUMEN_VIAE_API_BASE_URL` environment
variable; the Office client does not read it and always calls production.

## Design system

Every colour, gold included, comes from the active theme through `AppColors`;
never hardcode a hex. The exceptions are colours that are the same in every
theme — vestments, the sky at each hour, the mystery cards, the book cloths —
which live in `app/DesignSystem/FixedColors.swift`. The main tokens (full
palettes in `app/DesignSystem/Theme.swift`):

| Token | Marian Blue | Midnight | Candlelit (default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | `#0D1730` | `#131324` | `#0A0A15` |
| Card | `#17284E` | `#1F1F38` | `#151521` |
| Gold | `#D9B84A` | `#D4AF37` | `#E3BC5B` |
| Gold light | `#E9CC6E` | `#E8C547` | `#F3D89A` |
| Cream | `#F4EFE2` | `#F5F0E1` | `#F6F1E4` |
| Secondary text | `#93A5C8` | `#9C9CB5` | `#8B8BA5` |

Type is Cinzel for display and EB Garamond for reading, wrapped by `AppFonts`;
long text goes through `ReadingText` and `PrayerText`. Icons are Phosphor
(`ph-*`), Christicons for the devotional glyphs (`ch-*`), and a few drawn for
the app (`lv-*`), in the asset catalog, drawn through `AppIcon`.
