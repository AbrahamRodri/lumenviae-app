# Lauda Sion, the sequence of Corpus Christi: all twenty-four stanzas,
# Amen and Alleluia. Latin from the score (its "vacíles" kept as printed);
# English plain and literal. The cantor breathes inside "Lauda ducem et
# pastórem" and inside "In qua mensæ prima recólitur", where Whisper heard
# too little to place the breath (~); he sings on without a breath from
# "Sit laus plena" into "Sit jucúnda" and from "Quantum isti" into "Nec
# sumptus", which stand as one line each.

Lauda Sion Salvatórem, | Praise, O Sion, thy Saviour,
Lauda ducem et pastórem, | praise thy leader and thy shepherd
~
In hymnis et cánticis. | in hymns and canticles.
Quantum potes, tantum aude: | As much as thou canst, so much dare,
Quia major omni laude, | for He is greater than all praise,
Nec laudáre súfficis. | nor art thou able to praise Him enough.
Laudis thema speciális, | A theme of special praise,
Panis vivus et vitális | the living and life-giving Bread,
Hódie propónitur. | is set before us this day.
Quem in sacræ mensa cœnæ, | That at the table of the holy Supper
Turbæ fratrum duodénæ | to the band of the twelve brethren
Datum non ambígitur. | it was given, none doubteth.
Sit laus plena, sit sonóra, Sit jucúnda, sit decóra | Let praise be full, let it be resounding, let it be joyful, let it be comely,
Mentis jubilátio. | the jubilation of the mind.
Dies enim solémnis ágitur, | For the solemn day is kept
In qua mensæ prima recólitur | on which is recalled the first
~
Hujus institútio. | institution of this table.
In hac mensa novi Regis, | At this table of the new King,
Novum Pascha novæ legis, | the new Pasch of the new law
Phase vetus términat. | endeth the old Passover.
Vetustátem nóvitas, | The new puts the old to flight,
Umbram fugat véritas, | the truth puts the shadow to flight,
Noctem lux elíminat. | the light drives out the night.
Quod in cœna Christus gessit, | What Christ did at the Supper,
Faciéndum hoc expréssit | this He bade be done
In sui memóriam. | in memory of Him.
Docti sacris institútis, | Taught by His holy ordinances,
Panem, vinum in salútis | bread and wine into the saving
Consecrámus hóstiam. | Victim we consecrate.
Dogma datur christiánis, | This doctrine is given to Christians,
Quod in carnem transit panis, | that bread passeth into flesh,
Et vinum in sánguinem. | and wine into blood.
Quod non capis, quod non vides, | What thou dost not understand, what thou dost not see,
Animósa firmat fides, | a lively faith confirmeth,
Præter rerum órdinem. | beyond the order of nature.
Sub divérsis speciébus, | Under differing species,
Signis tantum, et non rebus, | signs only and not things,
Latent res exímiæ. | lie hidden wondrous realities.
Caro cibus, sanguis potus: | Flesh is meat, blood is drink;
Manet tamen Christus totus | yet Christ remaineth whole
Sub utráque spécie. | under either species.
A suménte non concísus, | By him who receiveth He is not cut,
Non confráctus, non divísus: | not broken, not divided;
Ínteger accípitur. | whole is He received.
Sumit unus, sumunt mille: | One receiveth, a thousand receive;
Quantum isti, tantum ille: Nec sumptus consúmitur. | as much as these, so much the one; nor, received, is He consumed.
Sumunt boni, sumunt mali: | The good receive, the wicked receive,
Sorte tamen inæquáli, | yet with unequal lot,
Vitæ vel intéritus. | of life or of destruction.
Mors est malis, vita bonis: | Death to the wicked, life to the good:
Vide paris sumptiónis | see, of a like receiving,
Quam sit dispar éxitus. | how unlike is the issue.
Fracto demum sacraménto, | When at last the Sacrament is broken,
Ne vacíles, sed meménto | waver not, but remember
Tantum esse sub fragménto, | that there is as much in a fragment
Quantum toto tégitur. | as is hidden in the whole.
Nulla rei fit scissúra: | No division is made of the substance;
Signi tantum fit fractúra, | the breaking is of the sign alone,
Qua nec status, nec statúra | by which neither the state nor the stature
Signáti minúitur. | of the One signified is lessened.
Ecce panis Angelórum, | Behold the Bread of Angels,
Factus cibus viatórum: | made the food of wayfarers,
Vere panis filiórum, | truly the bread of children,
Non mitténdus cánibus. | not to be cast to the dogs.
In figúris præsignátur, | In figures it is foreshown,
Cum Ísaac immolátur, | when Isaac is offered,
Agnus Paschæ deputátur, | the Paschal Lamb appointed,
Datur manna pátribus. | manna given to the fathers.
Bone pastor, panis vere, | Good Shepherd, true Bread,
Jesu, nostri miserére: | Jesus, have mercy on us;
Tu nos pasce, nos tuére, | do Thou feed us, defend us,
Tu nos bona fac vidére | do Thou make us see good things
In terra vivéntium. | in the land of the living.
Tu qui cuncta scis et vales, | Thou who knowest and canst all things,
Qui nos pascis hic mortáles: | who feedest us here as mortals,
Tuos ibi commensáles, | Thy fellow guests there,
Coherédes et sodáles | fellow heirs and companions
Fac sanctórum cívium. | make us of the holy citizens.
Amen. | Amen.
Allelúia. | Alleluia.
