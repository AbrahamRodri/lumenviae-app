# Omni die dic Mariae (Mode I), all twelve stanzas and the Amen.
# This cantor breathes at every quarter of a stanza, so a line is a quarter,
# but for the last half of the third stanza, sung in one breath.
# Latin from the score; English a plain, literal rendering.

Omni die dic Maríæ, | Every day speak to Mary,
Mea, laudes, ánima: | O my soul, her praises;
Ejus festa, ejus gesta | her feasts and her deeds
Cole splendidíssima. | keep with all splendour.
Contempláre et miráre | Behold and wonder at
Ejus celsitúdinem, | her exaltation;
Dic felícem Genitrícem, | call the Mother happy,
Dic beátam Vírginem. | call the Virgin blessed.
Ipsam cole, ut de mole | Honour her, that from the weight
Críminum te líberet: | of sins she may free thee;
Hanc appélla, ne procélla Vitiórum súperet. | call on her, lest the storm of vices overcome thee.
Propter Hevam homo sævam | Because of Eve, man received
Accépit senténtiam: | a cruel sentence;
Per Maríam habet viam | through Mary he has the way
Quæ ducit ad pátriam. | that leads to the homeland.
Clemens audi tuæ laudi | Gracious one, hear those
Quos instántes cónspicis: | whom thou seest intent upon thy praise;
Munda reos et fac eos | cleanse the guilty, and make them
Bonis dignos cælicis. | worthy of the good things of heaven.
Virga Jesse, spes oppréssæ | Rod of Jesse, hope and refuge
Mentis et refúgium, | of the burdened mind,
Decus mundi, lux profúndi, | glory of the world, light of the deep,
Dómini sacrárium. | sanctuary of the Lord.
Gemma decens rosa recens, | Comely jewel, fresh rose,
Castitátis lílium: | lily of chastity,
Castum chorum ad polórum | thou who leadest the chaste choir
Quæ perdúcis gáudium. | to the joy of heaven.
Pulchra tota, sine nota | All fair, without the mark
Cujuscúmque máculæ, | of any stain,
Fac me mundum et jucúndum | make me clean and glad
Te laudáre sédule. | to praise thee faithfully.
Ut sim castus et modéstus, | That I may be chaste and modest,
Dulcis, blandus, sóbrius, | gentle, kind and sober,
Pius, rectus, circumspéctus, | devout, upright and prudent,
Simultátis néscius: | a stranger to strife,
Erudítus et munítus | taught and armed
Divínis elóquiis, | by the words of God,
Timorátus et ornátus | God-fearing and adorned
Sacris exercítiis. | with holy exercises.
Virgo sancta, cerne quanta | Holy Virgin, see how many
Perferámus júgiter | we endure without end,
Tentaménta, et susténta | the temptations; and uphold
Nos ut stemus fórtiter. | us, that we may stand firm.
Esto tutrix et adjútrix | Be the guardian and helper
Christiáni pópuli; | of the Christian people;
Pacem præsta, ne molésta | grant peace, lest troubled
Nos pertúrbent sæcula. | times disturb us.
Amen. | Amen.
